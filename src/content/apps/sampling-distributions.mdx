    <p><b>Tutorial mode.</b> On your first visit the app opens one step at a time, with a guide telling you what to try next. Turn it off at the top right to see every control at once (your choice is remembered on this device).</p>
    <p><b>Population: cards.</b> It starts as A through 10 in every suit (40 cards, every value equally likely). Tap cards to add them to or remove them from the population. Each card's value is its number: ace = 1, J/Q/K = 10. Samples draw n cards <i>with replacement</i> (each card goes back before the next draw).</p>
    <p><b>Population: dice.</b> The population is one roll of a die. Use + and − under each face to load the die (for example, a die that rolls 6 half the time), or pick a preset. A sample is a roll of n dice (2 to start), and the histogram shows each roll's sum under its average.</p>
    <p><b>Predict, then reveal.</b> Compare the mean and SD of your x̄'s with what theory says: mean = μ and standard error = σ/√n.</p>
    <p><b>z-scores.</b> Turn on <i>show z-scores</i> to see z = (x̄ − μ) / (σ/√n) for each sample. Samples with |z| &gt; 2 are flagged. Tap any sample to see its average and z-score worked out, and hover a histogram bar to outline its samples.</p>
    <p><b>Compare.</b> Changing n or the population saves the current distribution in a table below the histogram. Open one to keep drawing, or press <i>compare</i> to see their histograms stacked. How does the SD of x̄ change when n gets 4 times bigger? How many dice does it take before a loaded die's averages look normal?</p>
